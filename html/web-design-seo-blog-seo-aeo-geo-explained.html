SEO, AEO and GEO are often sold as three separate disciplines. For most small businesses, that creates more confusion than value. The useful question is simpler: what makes your website easy to discover, understand, trust and cite across both traditional and generative search?

SEO is still the foundation

Google’s 2026 guidance says its generative search features remain rooted in core Search ranking and quality systems. That means technical accessibility, helpful content, internal links, page experience and clear business information still matter. You do not need to rebuild your site around an entirely separate “AI SEO” layer.

What does AEO mean?

AEO usually means Answer Engine Optimisation. In practice, it is about answering customer questions clearly enough that a search or answer system can identify the useful passage. That encourages strong headings, concise direct answers, supporting detail and evidence — all of which also improve usability for humans.

What does GEO mean?

GEO usually means Generative Engine Optimisation. The goal is visibility in AI-generated search experiences. Useful work includes making your brand/entity clear, publishing non-commodity expertise, earning genuine external references and ensuring important content is crawlable in text form.

What you do not need

Google explicitly says special AI text files, special schema and rewriting everything into tiny “AI chunks” are not requirements for its generative Search features. Structured data still matters for normal search understanding and eligible rich features, but there is no magic GEO schema.

A practical plan for a small business

Start with one authoritative page for each real service. Add genuinely useful local pages for the areas you actually serve. Publish articles that answer customer questions from first-hand experience. Keep your Google Business Profile accurate. Use Search Console and analytics to measure discovery and enquiries, then improve the pages that are closest to revenue.
