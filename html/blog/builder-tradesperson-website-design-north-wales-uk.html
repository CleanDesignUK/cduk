What should a construction or trades website include?

A good construction or trades website should clearly describe the services offered, show genuine completed work, explain the areas covered, display accurate business information and make requesting a quote easy. Mobile-friendly design, local SEO and a useful enquiry form are also important.

Many tradespeople build successful businesses through recommendations, repeat customers and word of mouth. These are valuable sources of work, but potential customers may still search online before choosing who to contact.

Homeowners looking for a roofer in Anglesey, plasterer in Bangor, builder in Caernarfon or electrician in Wrexham need to understand what the business does, where it works and whether it handles their type of project.

A professional website gives your construction company or trade business a place to answer those questions, demonstrate previous work and receive enquiries directly.

Why Builders and Tradespeople Need a Professional Website

Facebook pages, local directories and recommendation websites can help people discover a business. However, your own website lets you organise information around your services and projects.

You can explain the work you specialise in, show photographs from previous jobs, provide service area details and offer a direct quotation form.

A website is also useful when a potential customer has been referred by someone else. Rather than relying on a few social media photographs, they can review your services and past work in one place.

7 Features Every Construction and Trades Website Should Consider

1. Individual Pages for Your Main Services

Do not expect one short paragraph on a homepage to explain every service your company provides.

A building company may offer extensions, renovations, loft conversions and general building work. A roofing business may specialise in flat roofs, roof repairs, replacements and guttering.

Each main service can have a page that explains what is involved, the type of work offered and how customers can request an estimate.

This also creates useful opportunities to target relevant service searches, provided the pages contain meaningful information.

2. A Gallery of Completed Projects

Construction and trade websites are particularly well suited to visual portfolios.

Before-and-after photographs, finished work and project descriptions can give prospective customers a better understanding of what your business does.

Where possible, explain the type of project, service delivered and general location. Only publish photographs where you have the appropriate permissions.

3. A Proper Quotation Request Form

A quotation form should make it easy for a customer to explain what they need.

Useful information may include:

  • Name and contact details.
  • Type of building or trade service required.
  • Town or postcode of the project.
  • A brief description of the work.
  • Preferred timeframe.
  • Optional photographs of the relevant area.

The form should be simple enough to complete on a mobile phone. More detailed information can be collected during the quotation process.

4. Clear Service Area Information

If you work across Anglesey, Gwynedd, Bangor and Caernarfon, explain your real coverage.

If you also take projects in Conwy, Denbighshire, Flintshire or Wrexham, visitors should be able to identify that easily.

The same principle applies to construction companies working throughout Wales or across the UK.

5. Genuine Reviews, Qualifications and Accreditations

Customers may compare several companies before requesting a quote, particularly for higher-value home improvement projects.

Genuine customer reviews, relevant experience and appropriate evidence of qualifications can help them make a more informed decision.

Display only accreditations, registrations or memberships your business actually holds. If a service requires a particular legal registration, the information must be accurate and applicable.

6. A Mobile-Friendly Website

A customer might find your website while standing outside their property, researching a repair or comparing local contractors on their phone.

Make the navigation clear, avoid tiny buttons and ensure quotation forms are comfortable to use on smaller screens.

7. Clear Contact Details and Calls to Action

Visitors should never have to hunt for the quotation button.

Service pages can include a clear invitation to request a quote, while project galleries can link to the relevant service and contact information.

Local SEO for Builders and Tradespeople in North Wales

Local SEO helps search engines understand which services and locations are relevant to your business.

For a business operating in North Wales, useful service-related searches might include:

  • Builder in Anglesey.
  • Plasterer in Bangor.
  • Roofing contractor in Gwynedd.
  • House extension builder in Caernarfon.
  • Electrician in Wrexham.
  • Flat roof repair in North Wales.
  • Rendering services in Conwy.
  • Construction company in Wales.

These examples show the types of service and location searches customers may make. Your website should only target services and locations that accurately reflect your business.

Should Builders Have Separate Location Pages?

Location pages may be useful when they provide meaningful information about the services available in a specific area.

A construction business completing genuine projects across Gwynedd and Anglesey could include relevant project examples and service information.

Creating dozens of near-identical town pages with no distinct value is not a good long-term SEO strategy.

Can Construction Websites Be Found Through AI Search?

Search experiences are changing, but the underlying need for accurate, useful information remains important.

Clear service descriptions, verifiable business details, project information and relevant FAQs can help people and search systems understand your company.

There is no guaranteed method for making a website appear in an AI-generated recommendation.

How Much Does a Construction or Trades Website Cost?

Website prices depend on the complexity of the design, the number of services, the content required and any additional functionality.

A sole plasterer or decorator may need a straightforward website with services, photos and contact information.

A larger building company may need individual service pages, a detailed portfolio, wider location coverage and a more involved enquiry process.

When comparing website quotations, ask what is included and whether the arrangement covers:

  • Mobile-responsive design.
  • Core service pages.
  • Project or photo galleries.
  • Contact and quotation forms.
  • Basic SEO setup and page metadata.
  • Hosting, domains and ongoing costs.
  • Content changes and maintenance support.
  • Any extra booking or CRM functionality.

For businesses working with a limited budget, it may be sensible to begin with the most important services and expand over time.
