Where Should a Small Business Start with Digital Marketing?

Learn how to choose the right marketing priorities for your budget, from local SEO and Google Ads to social media, business content and generating customer enquiries.

What is the best way to start digital marketing?

Start by identifying your target customers, your main business goal and the actions you want customers to take. Make sure your website can support enquiries or sales, then prioritise the marketing channels most relevant to your audience and budget.

Digital marketing can feel overwhelming when you are running a small business. There are websites, Google Ads, SEO, Instagram, Facebook, email marketing and many other options competing for attention.

The answer is not to launch every channel immediately. A local services business in Anglesey may need a different approach from a growing ecommerce company or a corporate service provider working throughout the UK.

1. Decide What You Want Marketing to Achieve

Start with a clear commercial objective. You might want more local service enquiries, more online purchases, more bookings or greater awareness among a specific audience.

A clear goal helps determine which channels are suitable and how to measure success. For example, a consultancy seeking qualified business enquiries may prioritise different content from a local shop promoting an upcoming sale.

2. Make Sure Your Website Is Ready

Marketing attracts attention, but your website needs to explain what your business offers. If visitors cannot find the right service, understand your offer or contact you easily, additional traffic may not solve the problem.

Review the main pages, mobile layout, contact forms, booking options and the clarity of the information.

Our guide to designing a good small business website covers these foundations in more detail.

3. Consider Google Search and Local Visibility

For many service businesses, appearing when someone searches for the service is an important marketing opportunity.

Local SEO may be particularly relevant to businesses serving Bangor, Gwynedd, Holyhead, Conwy and other areas of North Wales.

Eligible businesses can also improve the information presented in their Google Business Profile, including opening hours, contact details, services and suitable photos.

Search optimisation usually requires ongoing attention rather than a one-off change to a page title.

4. Choose Social Media Platforms Carefully

Social media can help businesses communicate with existing customers, demonstrate their work and introduce their services to new audiences.

The right platform depends on where your customers spend time. Visual products may lend themselves to image-led content, while business-to-business services may use more educational and professional content.

Try to produce material that answers real customer questions. Useful project examples, product demonstrations, practical advice and service explanations can be more valuable than repeatedly publishing generic promotional messages.

5. Evaluate Google Ads and Paid Social Advertising

Paid advertising can help a business reach selected audiences or searches, but results depend on competition, targeting, budget, the offer and the destination page.

Before spending money, decide how you will recognise a useful enquiry or sale. Check that conversion tracking, where used, is implemented appropriately and respects applicable privacy requirements.

Ads should also send visitors to a relevant page. Someone searching for a specific service should not have to navigate through several unrelated pages to find it.

The best marketing channel is the one that reaches your customers and supports a useful business outcome, not simply the one generating the most views.

6. Plan a Realistic Budget and Review Results

Small businesses often need to work within a limited budget. Focus first on improving the most important customer journey rather than dividing a small budget across too many unrelated activities.

Measure enquiries, booked appointments, purchases and other meaningful actions. Compare performance over time, taking account of seasonality and changes in demand.

Small Business Marketing Starting Checklist

  • Identify the customers you want to reach.
  • Choose a measurable business goal.
  • Review your website and enquiry process.
  • Check local search visibility where relevant.
  • Prioritise one or two suitable channels.
  • Set a realistic content or campaign budget.
  • Review business outcomes, not only impressions.

Digital Marketing FAQs

What digital marketing should a small business prioritise?

It depends on the business and its customers. Local services may prioritise SEO and Google visibility, while ecommerce businesses may focus on product discovery, email marketing and paid campaigns.

Is digital marketing useful for businesses in North Wales?

Yes. Businesses in Anglesey, Bangor, Gwynedd, Conwy and Wrexham can use digital marketing to reach relevant local customers and wider audiences.

Should I use SEO or Google Ads?

SEO and Google Ads serve different purposes. SEO develops organic search visibility, while Google Ads provides paid advertising placements. Some businesses benefit from using both.
