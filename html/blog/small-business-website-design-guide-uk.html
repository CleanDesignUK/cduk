What Makes a Good Small Business Website?

Whether you run a local trades business in Anglesey, a tutoring company in Bangor or a growing UK service business, your website should make it easy for customers to understand what you offer and take the next step.

What makes a business website effective?

A good business website explains the company's services, establishes trust, works well on mobile devices and gives visitors a clear way to enquire, book or buy. It should also have useful content, straightforward navigation and sound technical SEO foundations.

A professional website can help customers find your business, compare your services and decide whether to contact you. However, attractive colours and images alone are not enough.

The most effective structure depends on what the business sells and what customers need to know. A local plasterer has different needs from an online retailer or training provider.

1. Include the Pages Customers Actually Need

Most small businesses benefit from a clear homepage, detailed service pages, an about page and a contact page. Additional pages should serve a genuine customer need.

  • Homepage: Explain what your business does, who it helps and where it operates.
  • Service pages: Give each important service enough space for useful details.
  • About page: Explain your experience, approach and business background.
  • Portfolio or examples: Show relevant work where you have permission to share it.
  • Contact page: Make contacting or booking with your business straightforward.
  • Legal information: Include appropriate privacy, cookie and other information where required.

A service business covering North Wales should make its real service area clear. A company working in both Anglesey and Gwynedd can explain that coverage naturally, without creating dozens of near-identical pages.

2. Make Mobile Usability a Priority

Potential customers may visit your website from a phone while travelling, at work or comparing several businesses. A responsive design should adapt to different screen sizes.

Check that headings do not overflow, buttons can be tapped comfortably, contact forms are easy to complete and the navigation works without frustration.

Website performance also matters. Oversized images, unnecessary scripts and poorly configured media can make a site slower than it needs to be.

3. Make Enquiries and Bookings Easy

Think about what should happen when a visitor decides they are interested. Should they send an enquiry, request a quote, schedule a call, book a lesson or purchase a product?

Your website should guide them towards the relevant action. For example, a tutoring company may benefit from tutor profiles and a lesson enquiry form, while a trades business may need a quotation request that collects project details.

Avoid asking for unnecessary information. A contact form should collect enough to respond to the customer without creating barriers to getting in touch.

The purpose of a business website is not simply to look professional. It should help the right customers understand your business and take action.

4. Build Search Visibility into the Website

Search engine optimisation should be considered when planning the website, not added as an afterthought.

Each important page should have a descriptive title, a clear main heading and content that genuinely answers the questions visitors are likely to ask.

Technical foundations include appropriate HTML structure, crawlable links, sensible URLs, mobile usability and correct canonical tags.

Businesses in Bangor, Caernarfon, Holyhead and elsewhere across North Wales may also benefit from clearly explaining the communities and areas they serve. This should reflect real service coverage.

5. Check the Website Before Launch

Small Business Website Launch Checklist

  • Every important page has a clear purpose.
  • Service descriptions are accurate and useful.
  • Navigation works on desktop and mobile.
  • Contact forms and booking links are tested.
  • Images have appropriate alternative text.
  • Page titles and descriptions are unique.
  • Canonical URLs point to the correct pages.
  • Privacy and cookie information is reviewed.
  • The live website uses HTTPS.
  • Broken links and missing images are checked.

A website is also an ongoing business asset. Services change, staff change and contact information needs updating. Choose a setup that you can maintain as your business grows.

Small Business Website FAQs

How many pages does a small business website need?

There is no fixed number. Many small businesses start with a homepage, about page, service pages and contact page. The correct structure depends on how many services you offer and what customers need to know.

Does a business website need SEO?

SEO-friendly foundations help search engines discover and understand the website. Useful page content, descriptive titles, mobile usability and technically accessible pages are sensible starting points.

Can a website help a local Anglesey business get enquiries?

A clear website can make it easier for customers to understand your services, see your coverage area and send an enquiry. The number of enquiries depends on factors including demand, competition, visibility and the offer itself.
