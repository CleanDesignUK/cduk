The 2026 Website Redesign Checklist for UK Businesses

A practical website redesign checklist for UK businesses covering SEO migration, content, conversion, Core Web Vitals, analytics, accessibility and launch checks.

In this guide 1. Audit before changing anything 2. Fix the architecture before the visuals 3. Rewrite for clarity, not keyword density 4. Simplify conversion paths 5. Protect technical SEO 6. Run a proper launch checklist
Published 12 October 2026 9 min read

A redesign can improve a website — or quietly remove the pages, rankings and conversion paths that were already working. The safest approach is to treat a redesign as a controlled migration rather than a visual reset.

1. Audit before changing anything

List the pages that receive organic traffic, enquiries, backlinks or meaningful engagement. Identify current titles, canonicals, forms, redirects, analytics tags and indexed URLs. If a page is valuable, decide deliberately whether it is staying, merging or redirecting.

2. Fix the architecture before the visuals

Group services into clear hubs and supporting pages. Make important pages reachable through internal links. Decide which location pages are genuinely useful rather than generating a page for every town name.

3. Rewrite for clarity, not keyword density

Keep the phrases customers use, but write primarily to answer the decision they are making. Service pages should explain who the service is for, what is included, where it is delivered, what happens next and why the business is credible.

4. Simplify conversion paths

A redesign is a good moment to remove unnecessary form fields, improve mobile input types, add clear validation and make phone/WhatsApp routes obvious. Track the action that matters rather than celebrating page views.

5. Protect technical SEO

Use one canonical URL per page, preserve strong URLs when possible, add 301 redirects when URLs change, generate an XML sitemap and ensure robots.txt is not blocking important sections. Structured data should match what users can actually see.

6. Run a proper launch checklist

Test forms, menus, internal links, mobile layouts, metadata, indexability, analytics consent, 404s and redirects. Submit the new sitemap in Search Console and watch coverage, impressions and conversions after launch instead of assuming the job ended on go-live day.
